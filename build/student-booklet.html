1Collecting Like TermsLearn
🎯 I can add and subtract like terms, e.g. 3a + 4a = 7a

Like terms have exactly the same letter part. You can only add or subtract like terms.

Like terms ✔NOT like terms ✘
3a and 5a3a and 5b (different letters)
x and 7xx and 7 (one has no letter)
2x2 and x2x2 and x (x2 is not the same as x)
4ab and abab and a
Think of the letters as objects. 3 apples + 4 apples = 7 apples, so 3a + 4a = 7a.
But 3 apples + 2 bananas is just 3 apples + 2 bananas, so 3a + 2b stays as 3a + 2b.
A letter on its own means 1 of it. a is the same as 1a. So a + a + a = 3a.

Worked examples

3a + 4a
3 lots of a plus 4 lots of a
= 7a
6x − 2x
6 take away 2 is 4
= 4x
5y − y
y means 1y, and 5 − 1 = 4
= 4y
2a + 3b + 4a + b
The a's: 2a + 4a = 6a
The b's: 3b + b = 4b
= 6a + 4b
x2 + 3x + 2x2
The x2's: x2 + 2x2 = 3x2
The x's: just 3x
= 3x2 + 3x
7m + 2 − 3m
The m's: 7m − 3m = 4m
The numbers: just 2
= 4m + 2
Remember: the sign (+ or −) in front of a term belongs to that term. Highlight or circle each type of term in a different colour before you add them up.

2Multiplying AlgebraLearn
🎯 I can multiply terms, e.g. 2a × 3b = 6ab and x × x = x2

In algebra we don't write the × sign. Letters and numbers that are multiplied are just written next to each other, number first.

We write……it means
3a3 × a
aba × b
5xy5 × x × y
x2x × x (say "x squared")
How to multiply terms
  1. Multiply the numbers together.
  2. Write the letters after the number (in alphabetical order).
  3. If the same letter appears twice, write it as a square: x × x = x2.

Worked examples

4 × a
Number first, then the letter
= 4a
a × b
Write the letters together
= ab
2a × 4
Numbers: 2 × 4 = 8
Letters: a
= 8a
2a × 3b
Numbers: 2 × 3 = 6
Letters: a and b
= 6ab
x × x
The same letter twice is "squared"
= x2
3x × 2x
Numbers: 3 × 2 = 6
Letters: x × x = x2
= 6x2
Remember: when you multiply, the numbers multiply (3 × 2 = 6) — don't add them! And x × x is x2, not 2x.
Going further: x × x × x = x3 ("x cubed"), and x2 × x = x3.

3Dividing AlgebraLearn
🎯 I can divide terms, e.g. 8x ÷ 2 = 4x and 6ab ÷ 2a = 3b

Dividing is the opposite of multiplying. A division can also be written as a fraction: 6a ÷ 2 is the same as 6a2.

How to divide terms
  1. Divide the numbers.
  2. Cancel any letter that is on the top and the bottom.
  3. Write down what is left.
Why can letters cancel? Anything divided by itself is 1. Just like 5 ÷ 5 = 1, a ÷ a = 1. So aba = b.

Worked examples

6a ÷ 2
Numbers: 6 ÷ 2 = 3
Letters: a stays
= 3a
ab ÷ a
The a cancels
b is left
= b
8x ÷ x
The x cancels
8 is left
= 8
12ab ÷ 4a
Numbers: 12 ÷ 4 = 3
a cancels, b is left
= 3b
x2 ÷ x
x2 is x × x
One x cancels, one is left
= x
6x2 ÷ 3x
Numbers: 6 ÷ 3 = 2
Letters: x2 ÷ x = x
= 2x
Remember: if all the letters cancel you are left with just a number, e.g. 5a ÷ a = 5. Write x2 as x × x if it helps you see what cancels.

4Expanding BracketsLearn
🎯 I can expand brackets, e.g. 3(x + 2) = 3x + 6

Expanding means getting rid of the brackets by multiplying. A number or letter right in front of a bracket means multiply everything inside the bracket by it.

Part 1: One bracket

Multiply the outside by EVERY term inside. Draw arrows so you don't miss one!
3(x + 2)  →  3 × x and 3 × 2  →  3x + 6
4(a + 5)
4 × a = 4a
4 × 5 = 20
= 4a + 20
2(x − 3)
2 × x = 2x
2 × 3 = 6, keep the minus
= 2x − 6
x(x + 5)
x × x = x2
x × 5 = 5x
= x2 + 5x

Part 2: Two brackets

Everything in the first bracket multiplies everything in the second bracket. A grid makes it easy to keep track. Example: (x + 2)(x + 3)

×x+3
xx23x
+22x6

1. Put one bracket on top and one down the side.

2. Multiply to fill each box.

3. Add up all four boxes: x2 + 3x + 2x + 6

4. Collect the like terms in the middle:

(x + 2)(x + 3) = x2 + 5x + 6

Remember: the outside term multiplies every term inside — 3(x + 2) is 3x + 6, not 3x + 2. With two brackets, you should always get 4 boxes.

5Factorising into BracketsLearn
🎯 I can factorise, e.g. 2x + 6 = 2(x + 3)

Factorising is the opposite of expanding. We put the brackets back in.

2(x + 3)   — expand →   2x + 6        2x + 6   — factorise →   2(x + 3)

Part 1: Take out a common factor

  1. Find the biggest number (and/or letter) that goes into both terms. Write it outside the bracket.
  2. Divide each term by it. Write the answers inside the bracket.
  3. Check by expanding — you should get back to the question.
5a + 10
5 goes into both
5a ÷ 5 = a, 10 ÷ 5 = 2
= 5(a + 2)
6x − 9
3 goes into both
6x ÷ 3 = 2x, 9 ÷ 3 = 3
= 3(2x − 3)
x2 + 4x
x goes into both
x2 ÷ x = x, 4x ÷ x = 4
= x(x + 4)

Part 2: Two brackets

To factorise x2 + 5x + 6, find two numbers that multiply to the last number (6) and add to the middle number (5).

Multiply to 6Add to…
1 × 61 + 6 = 7 ✘
2 × 32 + 3 = 5 ✔

The two numbers are 2 and 3, so they go in the brackets:

x2 + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)

Check by expanding: (x + 2)(x + 3) = x2 + 3x + 2x + 6 = x2 + 5x + 6 ✔

Remember: always check by expanding your answer. The brackets can go in either order: (x + 3)(x + 2) is also correct.
